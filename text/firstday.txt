// Stage 1 - Outline:
// When: The morning after the night ashore, after the wreck of the boat on the reef.
// Why: Establish the resource management and worker allocation mechanics of the game. Strategy over story.
// What: The five friends have next to nothing; the player must get them through the first day and divide the work.
// How: Search the beach, ask the other families for help, or make do with what they have; then focus the work on the short or the long term.
// But: Nobody shares without payment, wood is scarce, food is scarce, and every hand spent on one thing is missing from another.
// Who: The five friends, including the player. The friends cannot agree, so the player allocates the work. The person with the life trait always forages, so the work is given out around them.
// Finally: The day's work is divided; the first exploration trip is planned (workinprogress/foraging, next day).
//
// Stage 2 - Structure:
// Main path: §1 -> §40 the work focus -> §50 or §60 -> the ending sections.
// Main decision in §1: search the beach, ask the other families for help, make do, or decide tomorrow (§70).
// §10 search the beach (+1 wood). §20 ask for help: refused, but planks for silver; §21 buy the planks (needs silver). §30 make do.
// Work focus in §40: short term (§50) or long term (§60 with wood / §68 without wood).
// Short term: B, C and D forage; with wood, A builds (§51); with no wood, A cuts wood (§58) or forages too (§59).
// Long term: A builds; choose the building (§60), then B's work (§61 -> §62 build / §63 cut wood), then D's work (cut wood or forage); endings §64-§67. C always forages.
// Long term without wood (§68): A and B cut wood, planning to build tomorrow; C and D forage.
// §70: the decision is put off; the friends explore the new land (the forest) and carry back what it gives:
// §71 berries = food first (+2 food, +1 wood, bounty -2), §72 deadfall branches = wood first (+2 wood, +1 food, bounty -1).
// Nobody is assigned to any work; they rest after coming back.
// Endings: choiceless sections end the dialog.
//
// Stage 5 - Prose: all section text rewritten in the final style; effects, choices and structure unchanged.

§1
// Sunrise over the reef and the wreck; the five friends gather and count what they have; main decision.
The sun rises out of the sea, red through the last of the rain.
The reef shows dark under the breaking water, and the boat lies on it, broken open, and every wave takes something more off her.
The night gave you the beach, and the morning gives you the same world again in better light: the sky rinsing itself pale over the water, the dunes steaming where the sun reaches them, the wreck patient on the reef, going on with the work of coming apart.
Along the shore the other families are already up, mending, carrying, keeping to their own. They survived the same sea you did, and they have nothing to spare on anyone else's morning.
You call your friends together: <A>, <B>, <C> and <D>.
The counting takes no time at all: <food> food, <weapons> weapons, <wood> wood, <silver> silver, a box of tools, two blankets.
Five people, and next to nothing. The tools are good, the blankets are dry, and that is the whole of the good news.
The talk starts at once: what is here, what can be got, and who will do what today.

§10
// The beach search: a few hours at the tide line; one stack of usable wood.
[var wood 1]
You walk the tide line, and the tide line keeps the morning's ledger.
Most of what the storm has left is ruin: boards soaked soft, rope gone to weed, a cooking pot with a hole in it, things that belonged to somebody and may yet. You step over them all. Behind you the wreck groans on the reef with every wave, patient in its coming apart.
But there is wood too: planks and ribs heavy enough to dry and use. You drag one stack of it above the tide line and stand a moment, looking down the beach at all the rest of it, in case the sea means to give more.
It does not. The sea has given what it will give.
You come back to the camp with salt on your hands and one stack of wood for your trouble, and the talk turns to the work of the day.

§20
// Asking for help: no food is shared, but planks are for sale; no begging or yelling changes anything.
You walk the camps, from family to family, and ask for help.
The answer is the same everywhere, and it comes without unkindness: no food to spare, nothing given without payment. Everyone here survived the same sea, and everyone counts what is left the same way, every morning, and a beach full of people with nothing cannot afford to be generous.
One woman, older than the rest, hears you out and names a price: two stacks of wooden planks, salvaged from the boat in the night, for one silver coin. She does not apologize for the price, and the price is not an insult. The planks are hers to sell, and the coin is yours to spend, and that is the whole of the bargain.
No begging moves anyone. No shouting moves anyone. On this beach, silver speaks, and nothing else does.

The coin leaves your hand and does not come back, and the planks come to the sand in its place: sound wood, cut and stacked in the night, edges square, worth more than the price to people with nothing.
The woman counts the coin once, nods once, and goes back to her family without a word wasted. A clean bargain is the one courtesy this beach still knows.
You carry the planks back above the tide line, two stacks of them, and stand them with your own things. It is strange, how much of a house is already in a pile of wood, waiting.
Back at the camp the talk turns to the work of the day.

§30
// Making do: no help asked for, nothing searched for; the work of the day waits.
You look at what you have and decide it will have to be enough.
Nobody here is coming to save you. Nobody is waiting to be asked. Nothing on this beach is free, and what is yours is what stands between you and the sea's patience.
So the friends keep to themselves and make do, and sit close around the box of tools in the lee of the dunes, out of the wind. The tools are good ones, and the hands around them are alive, and the day is still whole, and unwritten.
The talk turns to the work of the day.

§40
// The work focus: the main strategic decision of the day.
The friends do not agree on what the day is for.
"Food first," says <C>. "Hunger does not wait for walls. There is food out there for anyone who goes looking, and I am going looking."
"And when the weather turns again?" says <B>. "Tonight, or in ten days, it will turn. We were lucky once, and luck does not keep. Build first. What we raise this morning is what stands between us and the next storm."
They are both right, and that is the shape of the problem. A day spent foraging is a day nothing grows out of the sand. A day spent building is a day the food does not gather itself. Every hand given to one thing is missing from another.
<D> stands between the two of them and wants what <D> always wants: the five of you whole, and agreed. But the day will not wait for agreement either.
They look to you. They will go where the day is sent.

§70
// The alternate ending: the work decision is put off; the friends explore the new land, the forest beyond the dunes.
Not today.
The friends cannot agree on the work of the day, and you let the question stand: tomorrow, with clearer heads, and a day's worth of knowing the land behind the argument.
But they will not sit on the beach and wait, and neither will you, so the five of you go together to look at the new land. Past the dunes the sand gives way to grass, the grass to brush, and the brush to forest: green standing dark and quiet under the morning, the first real trees any of you have seen in weeks, and the sound of them is wind, and birds, and nothing else.
The forest is not empty. Berry bushes stand in the clearings, heavy with dark fruit. Fallen deadfall branches lie under the trees, dry enough to burn, which nothing on this beach has been since you landed.
Two things are worth carrying back, and there are hands enough for either.
