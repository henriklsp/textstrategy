// text/firstday.txt
// The first day ashore, after the shipwreck.
// Event prerequisites: none; firstday is a scheduled event, fired by
// [event firstday 1] at the end of text/intro.txt, so the first day begins the
// next morning, after the daily update.
//
// Stage 1 - Outline:
// When: The morning after the night ashore, after the wreck of the boat on the reef.
// Why: Establish the resource management and worker allocation mechanics of the game. Strategy over story.
// What: The group must agree on what matters most now and divide the day's work, with nothing but what they carried ashore.
// How: Talk it through, then assign who searches the surroundings for wood and materials.
// But: There is no wood and little food, and the other families share nothing; every choice leaves something undone.
// Who: The four friends: A talks of destiny and what is coming, B plans ahead, C insists on immediate needs, D works for agreement; their interests pull in different directions.
// Finally: The work of the day is divided and the first exploration trip is planned (workinprogress/foraging, next day).
//
// Stage 2 - Structure:
// Main path: §1 the morning after -> §30 who searches the surroundings -> §40 the end of the day.
// Main decision in §1: the friends pull in different directions and look to the
// player: plan for the long term, focus on immediate needs, or decide nothing.
// The dilemma: there is no wood and little food; planning comforts, scavenging feeds,
// and putting off the decision still ends with the day spent on food or wood.
// Consequences: §10 plan ahead, §20 immediate needs, §60 alternative path away
// from the main path: the friends reject idleness, the group explores the forest,
// and the day ends at §61 (berries) or §62 (branches).
// Endings: §40, §61 and §62 end the dialog with no choices.

*A: divinity
*B: magic
*C: life
*D: harmony

§1
// The morning after: the group takes stock; main decision.
// TODO: set the scene by opening with a dramatic description of the scenery and the sun rising, outlining the reefs and the boat.
The morning comes grey and wet.
Everyone has come ashore. Some of the others slept; you all did, in the end, in the huddle behind the dunes.
There is no fire, and there will be none this morning: nothing is dry enough to burn.
The wreck lies on the reef and breaks a little more with every wave.
The other families camp along the shore and keep to their own; nobody shares anything with anyone.
You have no wood, and little food, and no plan.
<A>, <B>, <C> and <D> gather around you.
<A> talks about destiny, and about one day ruling these lands.
<C> insists on the need right now to find food and materials.
<B> wants to plan ahead.
<D> tries to get them all to work together.
They do not agree, and look to you.

§10
// Consequence of planning ahead: agreement on the goal of a settlement, but nothing can be built without wood.
Everyone except <C> agrees on the need to plan ahead.
"We should decide what to build first," says <B>.
"There is nothing to build with," says <C>. "No wood, no dry rope, no tools but what we carry. First we gather, then we build."
The talk ends there, on first things first.
#30 Who should search the surroundings for wood and materials?

§20
// Consequence of immediate needs: C and D start scavenging right away.
[task C scavenge]
[task D scavenge]
<C> and <D> agree with you on the need to find food and materials quickly.
<A> and <B> reluctantly accept.
<C> and <D> start scavenging the beach and the dunes right away.
Still, someone should go further out and look for wood and materials.
#30 And who should search the surroundings for wood and materials?

§30

[set person=choice]
Whoever goes must pick over the dunes, the tide line and the grass beyond the camps, and bring back anything the sea or the land gives.
The others will hold the camp together and watch the water.
Who should search the surroundings for wood and materials?

§60
// Alternative path: the friends reject doing nothing; the obvious next step is to
// explore the new land; in the forest they find berry bushes and fallen branches; a decision.
You decide nothing today.
The others will not have it.
"There is nothing to eat," says <C>. "Standing here will not change that."
"Then we look at what this land will give," says <B>.
<D> nods and stands up. Even <A> wants to see what lies beyond the dunes.
You walk inland together, and the beach gives way to grass, and the grass to forest.
The forest gives: berry bushes heavy with fruit, and branches fallen from the trees, dry enough to burn.
There is time for one, not both.
#61 Spend the day gathering berries
#62 Spend the day gathering branches

§61
// Ending: the day goes to the berries; food over wood; bounty falls with the food scavenged.
[var food 2]
[var wood 1]
[var bounty -2]
[event workinprogress/foraging 1]
You spend the day among the berry bushes with the others, picking into every kind of container anyone can find.
You eat as you pick, the first meal worth the name since the boat.
On the way back you each carry a few branches as well, but fewer.
The camp eats that night, and the bushes will need time to give again.

§62
// Ending: the day goes to the branches; wood over food; bounty falls with the food scavenged.
[var wood 2]
[var food 1]
[var bounty -1]
[event workinprogress/foraging 1]
You spend the day on what the trees have dropped: snapping the fallen branches, dragging them clear, binding them in bundles you can carry.
You pick berries as you go, and eat more of them than you bring back.
By evening there is wood stacked beside the camp, a little food for the night, and the bushes will need time to give again.
