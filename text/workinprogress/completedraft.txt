§1
Morning comes up over the camp, and the raft lies finished above the tide line, lashed planks and a rough mast-step, crude and whole. You put a hand on it and it does not shift. The others come down to look, and nobody says anything for a while, because there it is: the first thing on this coast that you have built rather than salvaged or buried, and the first thing here that floats.
"So," one of them says at last, looking at the raft and then out at the water. "Now do we go anywhere on it?"
That is the question of the day, and it is yours to answer.

§2
You call them together there above the tide line, and the talk starts at once: what to raise next, and who should decide it. Before long the talk has made itself a council, the way talks do, and you sit down to it properly.

§3
There is no hurry in the morning, and you let it stay that way. The raft is hauled up past the tide line to wait. The work of the day sorts itself out the way it always does, and the deciding can wait for a day when deciding is needed.
